When you swallow a supplement, have you ever wondered how much of it your body actually uses?

The answer depends on several factors, including the ingredient, formulation, dose and how your body processes it.

This is where absorption science becomes important.

## What Is Bioavailability?

Bioavailability describes how much of a substance becomes available to the body and how quickly it does so.

After you swallow a product, its ingredients may need to dissolve, pass through the digestive system and cross the intestinal lining. Some substances are also broken down before reaching the bloodstream.

Consequently, the amount printed on a label does not necessarily equal the amount that reaches your circulation.

## Does the Formulation Matter?

Yes. The way a substance is formulated can influence how it dissolves, remains stable and is absorbed.

Different routes of administration also behave differently:

- **Oral:** The substance passes through the digestive system.
- **Intravenous:** The substance enters the bloodstream directly.
- **Intramuscular or subcutaneous:** The substance is administered into muscle or tissue beneath the skin.
- **Intranasal or inhaled:** The substance is delivered through the nasal passages or respiratory system.

Each route has specific uses, limitations and safety considerations. One is not automatically better than another.

## Does Better Absorption Mean Better Results?

Not necessarily.

A formulation might increase the amount of a substance measured in the bloodstream. That does not automatically prove it will improve health outcomes.

Researchers still need to investigate whether the substance reaches the intended site, produces meaningful benefits and has an acceptable safety profile.

Greater absorption can sometimes increase side effects as well.

## Ask Beyond the Label

When evaluating a supplement or health intervention, ask what evidence supports its formulation, whether absorption has been measured appropriately and whether the product has demonstrated meaningful benefits.
